Update Research note

13 OCT 2026

One Mountain After Another

NeurIPS, ICML, and ICLR remain distant, guiding stars in my mind. I still hold the hope of a stubborn dreamer; along the way, I may also publish the occasional paper in a CCF-A journal.

Yet the reality of my university and the school of management anchors me elsewhere. They place far greater weight on management journals—especially those on the UTD list—than on the pinnacles of pure computer science.

For now, the bulk of my time and energy is poured into management research, carrying my technical craft along like a quiet companion. A sense of regret inevitably follows: the questions that truly fascinate me, the puzzles I have yet to solve, must wait. They linger like ghosts—unsettled business I am not yet finished with.

But one mountain follows another.

"To climb steep hills requires slow pace at first." — Shakespeare

Since I have chosen to keep walking, there is no need to sigh for the road already taken. I will press forward, pausing only to look back and see how far I have already come.

    03Research

    Research directions.

    I study efficient learning from wearable sensor sequences and structured graph data, with an emphasis on robust representations and practical inference.

    Wearable Sensing & Activity Recognition

    Sensor-based human activity recognition with multimodal, multi-channel time-series signals in realistic, long-tailed settings.

    Efficient Neural Architectures

    Layer-wise and block-wise training, decoupled and re-parameterized convolutions, and accuracy–speed trade-offs for practical deployment.

    Attention for Time Series

    Temporal, spatial, channel, and cross-domain attention mechanisms for structured sensor intelligence.

    Data-Driven Dynamical Systems

    Neural sequence models that approximate and identify nonlinear governing equations directly from data.
